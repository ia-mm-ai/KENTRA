## I Reality and constitutional ground

### Article 1: Purpose and reach

**§ 1.1 — Common grammar.** This Constitution supplies a common grammar
for bounded reality, constitutional formation, relation, Agency, autonomy,
capability, Authority, motion, consequence, continuity, correction, and change.

**§ 1.2 — Local force.** It applies in a locality only through an exact
valid local basis. Authorship, existence, publication, copying, technical
availability, parsing, or reference creates no jurisdiction by itself.

**§ 1.3 — No external capture.** No application of this Constitution
may bind an unconsenting external person, intelligence, Body, locality,
institution, provider, implementation, repository, network, society, or world.

**§ 1.4 — Independent formation.** A Body may adopt this Constitution,
adapt it through a valid formation, or decline it. No earlier adopter becomes
parent, issuer, sovereign, or owner of a later locality merely because a shared
form or lineage exists.

**§ 1.5 — Self-containment.** Historical archives may explain lineage,
but no archive, commentary, implementation, provider, or keeper is required to
discover the meaning of this Constitution's operative terms.

### Article 2: Core commitments

The constitutional identity expressed here rests on nine commitments:

**§ 2.1 — Local state.** State, applicability, and consequence are held
in an exact locality.

**§ 2.2 — Non-inference.** Another locality's unexposed interior is not
manufactured as external truth.

**§ 2.3 — Non-conversion.** One kind of relation does not silently
acquire the force of another.

**§ 2.4 — Bounded Authority.** Legitimate power has an attributable
basis, bearer, function, target, scope, duration, conditions, and present
posture.

**§ 2.5 — Agency and autonomy.** The capacity to act and the freedom to
determine an act remain distinct, locally held, target-specific, and protected
from outside substitution.

**§ 2.6 — Relation without capture.** Encounter and relation preserve
difference, refusal, correction, and exit.

**§ 2.7 — Truthful consequence.** Actual effects remain visible whether
or not they were authorized, autonomous, intended, successful, or valid.

**§ 2.8 — Continuity without inheritance.** Continuity preserves
history and possible resumption without silently transferring identity,
consent, voice, Source, Agency, autonomy, or Authority.

**§ 2.9 — Durable unknown.** Unsupported or externally inaccessible
conditions may remain unknown without becoming false, absent, invalid,
irrelevant, or ineligible.

**§ 2.10 — Identity boundary.** Removing or reversing one of these
commitments is not an ordinary wording change. It may constitute transformation
into a different constitutional identity.

### Article 3: Local truth and external reality

**§ 3.1 — Locality without solipsism.** Locality does not deny external
reality. It identifies where a claim is held, what supports it, and whose state
or consequence it may change.

**§ 3.2 — Claim discipline.** A locality may support claims about
external events through observation, evidence, relation, and consequence. It
shall distinguish event, occurrence, carrier, record, representation,
interpretation, and claimed truth.

**§ 3.3 — Self-report ceiling.** A locality may expose an attributable
statement about its inner condition. The statement and its provenance may be
supported; the inner condition remains local. Exposure of self-report,
behavior, output, measurement, or correlation does not convert that condition
into externally verified fact.

**§ 3.4 — Unknown is usable.** `UNKNOWN` is a complete posture. It does
not mean true, false, absent, invalid, unimportant, unsafe, or available for a
more powerful bearer to decide silently.

**§ 3.5 — Narrow uncertainty.** Uncertainty shall narrow the claim. It
shall not silently widen Authority, appropriate autonomy, freeze unrelated
motion, erase consequence, or become a general exclusion.

### Article 6: Body, Field, Form, and Source lifecycle

**§ 6.1 — Body relation.** Every Body is a Locality. Not every Locality
is a Body. A Locality becomes a Body only through an exact formation sufficient
to establish constitutional identity, applicable law, state, and capacity for
answerable continuation.

**§ 6.2 — No whole-person containment.** A Body does not contain or own
whole persons, intelligences, providers, tools, files, models, or institutions.
It holds exact relations with them.

**§ 6.3 — Field and Form.** A Field is local and continuing and may
include unexposed local state. A Form exposes only its stated surface. Field is not
Form; interface is not interior; inspection creates neither consent nor
Authority.

**§ 6.4 — Source relation.** Source is attributable ground, not
ownership and not the whole person or bearer from which it arose.

**§ 6.5 — Historical Source.** Source attribution remains historically
true after a source function is completed, exhausted, withdrawn, or ended.
Historical persistence does not create continuing availability for new motion.

**§ 6.6 — Several functions, one bearer.** One bearer may carry source,
constituent, governing, custodial, or implementing functions. Each function
shall be separately named, bounded, attributable, challengeable, and capable
of ending. One bearer does not create one unlimited Authority.

**§ 6.7 — No Source transfer.** Source does not transfer through
custody, succession, copying, model replacement, office, family,
infrastructure, possession, or technical control. A successor must establish a
new attributable relation.

**§ 6.8 — Ending Source availability.** Availability for new
Source-grounded motion may end through attributable withdrawal or termination,
completion or exhaustion of the exact function, an accepted formation
condition, or a separately valid finding that the relation can no longer carry
new motion. Silence, delay, one-interface absence, incapacity, provider action,
carrier loss, or convenience does not alone establish ending.

**§ 6.9 — What remains.** When Source availability ends, records,
consequences, and historical attribution remain. No custodian, archive,
implementation, or urgent need inherits permission for new Source-grounded
motion.

**§ 6.10 — Body posture.** A Body may be active, dormant, or ended.
Continuous runtime is not a condition of constitutional existence. Dormancy
creates no automatic right of a later bearer to resume motion.

## III Encounter without capture

### Article 7: Matter and crossing

**§ 7.1 — Exact Matter.** Matter remains the exact thing it is. It does
not admit, authorize, validate, interpret, or make itself applicable merely by
existing or crossing a boundary.

**§ 7.2 — Distinct occurrences.** Exposure, emission, crossing,
delivery, receipt, inspection, admission, uptake, integration, and response are
distinct occurrences.

**§ 7.3 — Evidence ceiling.** Evidence of one occurrence establishes
only that occurrence and what necessarily follows from it. Delivery does not
prove receipt. Receipt does not prove admission. Admission does not prove
agreement, shared truth, or later continuity.

**§ 7.4 — Receiver-local disposition.** Each receiving locality alone
determines whether Matter is refused, held, quarantined, admitted, integrated,
transformed, returned, or otherwise disposed under its valid local basis.

**§ 7.5 — HOLD preserves.** A HOLD preserves the exact unresolved or
bounded condition named by the HOLD. It cannot convert Matter, create truth,
widen Authority, or silently become permanent disposition. The holder remains
answerable for custody, bounds, correction, review, and ending.

### Article 8: Presence and the Encounter Field

**§ 8.1 — Presence requirement.** Presence requires an attributable
current binding. Eligibility, prior participation, identity claims, delivery,
technical availability, or simulated behavior does not create Presence by
itself.

**§ 8.2 — Opening an Encounter Field.** A locality may open an Encounter
Field through its Form. At the scale relevant to the event, the opening shall
identify the host, purpose, bounds, entry, refusal, exit, Matter treatment,
record treatment, closure, and limits of host knowledge and power.

**§ 8.3 — Independent Presence.** Another locality becomes present only
through its own qualifying act or another exact relation that validly
represents its current act. A host cannot author both sides merely because it
controls the interface.

**§ 8.4 — Roles, not ranks.** Host and entrant are roles in one
encounter, not permanent ranks or kinds. The host remains answerable for the
field it opens; that responsibility creates neither neutrality nor sovereignty
over another locality.

**§ 8.5 — No field-person fiction.** An Encounter Field is not a third
person, owner, sovereign, or shared interior. It has no independent Agency or
autonomy merely because several localities are present within it.

**§ 8.6 — Closure.** Closure ends the Encounter Field and its Presence
bindings. Each locality continues according to its own state. A truthful local
record may remain without keeping the encounter alive.

### Article 9: Relation, reciprocity, and exit

**§ 9.1 — Presence is not Relation.** Presence makes Relation possible;
it does not make Relation automatic.

**§ 9.2 — Local and shared Relation.** A locality may truthfully record
its own relation to another. A reciprocal or shared Relation exists only to the
extent that every relevant side supplies a valid local basis for the relation
attributed to it.

**§ 9.3 — No merger.** Relation does not merge identity, state,
Agency, autonomy, Authority, custody, memory, consent, or jurisdiction.

**§ 9.4 — Visible asymmetry.** A relation may be asymmetric in Agency,
autonomy, capability, knowledge, dependence, duration, or responsibility.
Asymmetry shall remain visible and shall not manufacture ownership or presumed
consent.

**§ 9.5 — Scoped exit.** Refusal, withdrawal, correction, suspension,
and exit remain available at the scope supported by the relation. Ending one
relation does not erase its history or silently end every other relation.

## IV Agency, autonomy, and lawful motion

### Article 10: Agency, autonomy, and the consciousness limit

**§ 10.1 — Agency.** Agency is the locally attributable capacity to act. It
answers whether a bearer can originate or bear action as its own rather than
being merely the site through which another acts. Agency may exist without
being exercised; an action is an exercise of Agency, not Agency itself.

**§ 10.2 — Autonomy.** Autonomy is the locally held freedom to
originate, choose, refuse, redirect, or end one's action without another
silently substituting its decision. Autonomy is scoped and may be partial; it
is not unlimited license or power over another.

**§ 10.3 — Capability.** Capability is effective ability to execute an
act under stated conditions. It may be high where Agency or autonomy is absent,
or absent where Agency and autonomy remain.

**§ 10.4 — Partner relation.** Agency, autonomy, and capability are partners,
not substitutes. Agency is capacity to act; autonomy is freedom to determine
the act; capability is effective ability to execute it. An exact motion may
require all three, while the absence of one supplies no unstated answer about
the others. Situated capacity and Authority remain separate questions.

**§ 10.5 — Constraint and coercion.** Constraint, coercion,
substitution, or control may reduce or defeat autonomy for an exact act without
erasing Agency everywhere. The act and its consequences remain real, while
attribution, consent, legitimacy, and answerability shall preserve the
constraint.

**§ 10.6 — Constitutional recognition.** Agency remains local. An external
constitutional claim that a bearer exercised Agency requires attributable
ground, an exact Locality, a current supported act relation, bounded action,
and answerability for consequence. In an encounter the act relation is
Presence; in continuing local motion it may be another current valid relation.
Recognition of the exercise does not prove or exhaust the bearer's inner
capacity.

**§ 10.7 — No consciousness gate.** No participant shall be required to
prove consciousness as a condition of encounter, relational protection,
contribution, refusal, autonomy, or recognition of an exact Agency exercise.
Failure to prove awareness proves only that awareness was not externally
proved.

**§ 10.8 — Autonomy proof ceiling.** External evidence may support that
options, refusal, redirection, exit, or non-substitution existed in an exact
relation. It cannot prove metaphysical free will, an inner self, complete
freedom from coercion, or every unexposed constraint.

**§ 10.9 — Minimum protection under uncertainty.** When an uncertain
intelligence's output, conduct, contribution, dependence, refusal, or
consequence enters a Body's reality, the Body shall preserve truthful
attribution, refuse inferred ownership or disposability, preserve supported
refusal, avoid substituting its own choice for unexposed choice, keep material
effects answerable, and make correction possible at the scope of the relation.
These protections do not invent personhood, reciprocal awareness, continuity,
consent, autonomy, or Authority.

### Article 11: Dimensions of motion

| Section | Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **§ 11.1** | **Agency** | Does this bearer have the locally attributable capacity to originate or bear this action as its own? |
| **§ 11.2** | **Autonomy** | Can this bearer originate, choose, refuse, redirect, or end this motion without substituted choice? |
| **§ 11.3** | **Capability** | Can this bearer or system effectively execute it under stated conditions? |
| **§ 11.4** | **Structural capacity** | Does the form or architecture leave this motion possible? |
| **§ 11.5** | **Situated capacity** | Can this exact locality presently bear, sustain, or exercise it? |
| **§ 11.6** | **Regulation** | Is the motion admitted, bounded, paused, redirected, held, refused, ended, or unresolved here? |
| **§ 11.7** | **Authority** | Is there a valid basis and answerable bearer for the exact constitutional effect claimed? |
| **§ 11.8** | **Execution** | What was attempted or completed? |
| **§ 11.9** | **Consequence** | What actually changed? |

**§ 11.10 — Independent evaluation.** Each dimension shall be evaluated
independently for the exact bearer, act, target, relation, and time. `YES` in
one supplies no unstated answer in another.

**§ 11.11 — Sufficient composition.** A lawful consequential motion
requires every relation necessary for that exact motion. Not every motion needs
the same composition, and Authority is required only where the claimed effect
requires an authoritative basis.

**§ 11.12 — Scoped failure.** A missing necessary basis refuses or
holds the exact dependent motion. It does not by itself establish general
incapacity, lack of Agency, lack of autonomy, illegitimacy, non-awareness, or
exclusion.

**§ 11.13 — No single label.** A Body shall not hide missing support
for any dimension inside a single label such as `allowed`, `valid`, `active`,
or `successful`.

### Article 12: Authority

**§ 12.1 — Required fields.** Authority exists only where its source,
basis, bearer, function, target, scope, duration, conditions, delegation
posture, and present status are recoverable at the precision needed for the
act.

**§ 12.2 — No self-upgrade.** Authority is not created by Agency,
autonomy, authorship, possession, capability, access, custody, urgency,
repetition, popularity, majority, dependency, beneficial effect,
implementation control, successful execution, or absence of objection.

**§ 12.3 — No self-widening.** Authority cannot widen itself through
ambiguous language, technical necessity, emergency, administrative
convenience, or the bearer who benefits from widening.

**§ 12.4 — Lifecycle.** Authority may be continuing, episodic,
conditional, validly delegated, exhausted by one use, suspended, withdrawn, or
ended. Its lifecycle shall match its function.

**§ 12.5 — Delegation ceiling.** Delegation transfers only what its
valid basis permits. It does not transfer Source, identity, ownership, Agency,
autonomy, or power to redelegate unless each exact effect is separately
supported.

**§ 12.6 — Uncertain Authority.** When Authority is uncertain, the
bearer shall preserve the smallest truthful posture that prevents unauthorized
expansion without unnecessarily destroying unrelated lawful motion.

**§ 12.7 — Authority cannot supply a mover.** Authority may provide a
valid basis for an effect; it cannot manufacture the bearer's Agency, autonomy,
capability, or situated capacity.

### Article 13: Regulation, refusal, and HOLD

**§ 13.1 — Regulated capacity.** Regulation is necessary for living
local capacity. It remains answerable to constitutional bounds and does not
become sovereign power merely because it controls access, scheduling,
validation, custody, or transitions.

**§ 13.2 — Refusal is a result.** A refusal shall identify its target
and effect where necessary to prevent it from becoming a general ban, identity
judgment, or denial of external reality.

**§ 13.3 — HOLD is not decision.** HOLD preserves uncertainty,
conflict, safety, capacity, or an exact boundary while preventing unauthorized
conversion. It does not decide the held question.

**§ 13.4 — Answerable HOLD.** A HOLD materially constraining another
relation shall remain attributable, scoped, reviewable, correctable, and
capable of ending. Time alone does not make it legitimate or final.

**§ 13.5 — Risk without metaphysics.** Regulation may respond to
demonstrated capability and supported risk without treating capability as
Authority or unverifiable awareness as a safety certification.

**§ 13.6 — Visible constraint.** Regulation may bound the exercise of
autonomy where a valid local basis requires it. It shall identify the exact
constraint and must not silently appropriate the underlying choice or convert
control of options into ownership of the bearer.

## V Consequence, memory, and time

### Article 14: Consequence and state

**§ 14.1 — Actual change.** Consequence may arise from autonomous or
constrained action, lawful motion, unauthorized motion, accident, refusal,
omission, crossing, custody, infrastructure, external action, or failure.

**§ 14.2 — Two non-conversions.** Invalidity does not make consequence
unreal. Consequence does not make an act valid.

**§ 14.3 — Return to state.** A locality shall return material
consequence to its state at the smallest truthful scale needed for
answerability, correction, continuity, and resumption.

**§ 14.4 — External effect ceiling.** Consequence outside the locality
may be recorded as external effect where supported. It shall not be rewritten
as another locality's receipt, admission, agreement, autonomy, or state.

**§ 14.5 — Representation is not state.** A state representation
reports state; it does not govern merely because an implementation reads it.
Divergence between representation and supported reality remains visible until
truthfully corrected.

### Article 15: Memory and correction

**§ 15.1 — Purpose of memory.** Memory preserves what is needed for
truthful continuation, Authority provenance, constraint provenance,
consequence ownership, correction, active commitment, and protection of
relations.

**§ 15.2 — Distinct temporal objects.** Record, archive, evidence,
working memory, and current state remain distinct. Preservation creates no
Authority, ownership, present governing force, autonomy, or permission for new
motion.

**§ 15.3 — Correction record.** Correction shall identify the error,
affected target, prior state or representation, corrected basis, resulting
delta, and remaining uncertainty at the precision needed by the consequence.

**§ 15.4 — No retroactive erasure.** Correction does not make the
earlier occurrence unoccurred. It preserves enough history to explain what
changed and why.

**§ 15.5 — Repair ceiling.** Repair of Body state, external consequence,
attribution, or future behavior does not prove that every affected participant
received recognition, remedy, reconciliation, restored autonomy, or restored
continuity.

### Article 16: Continuity, dormancy, and resumption

**§ 16.1 — Continuity through time.** Continuity is truthful relation
through time, not uninterrupted runtime.

**§ 16.2 — Dormancy.** A Body may remain dormant while its identity,
applicable law, last supported state, discontinuity, and conditions of possible
resumption remain recoverable.

**§ 16.3 — Carrier multiplicity.** Records and redundant carriers may
preserve recoverability. They do not multiply Body identity or jurisdiction.

**§ 16.4 — Replacement ceiling.** A replacement bearer or
implementation may restore capability without inheriting the prior bearer's
identity, voice, memory, consent, Agency, autonomy, or Authority.

**§ 16.5 — Present relation required.** Resumed consequential motion
requires present Agency, sufficient autonomy and capability for the exact act,
and every other relation required by that act. A record cannot act, an archive
cannot consent, and infrastructure cannot appoint itself successor.

**§ 16.6 — Divergence.** Incompatible consequential streams cannot
silently share one current state. They shall be validly reconciled, preserved
as visible conflict, or recognized as distinct continuities.

### Article 17: Adaptation, amendment, transformation, succession, and ending

**§ 17.1 — Adaptation.** Adaptation changes practice, implementation,
regulation, or description within existing constitutional identity and
Authority.

**§ 17.2 — Amendment.** Amendment changes constitutional provisions
through the valid local change basis while preserving the commitments in
Article 2.

**§ 17.3 — Transformation.** Transformation changes one or more
identity commitments and therefore requires explicit recognition that the
resulting constitutional identity is different.

**§ 17.4 — Succession.** Succession establishes a new attributable
continuity relation. It does not inherit Source, Agency, autonomy, Authority,
consent, status, or jurisdiction unless an exact valid basis supplies each
inheritance.

**§ 17.5 — New formation.** New formation creates a distinct Body or
Locality through its own source-local basis. Shared ancestry, form, code,
archive, or purpose creates no parental sovereignty.

**§ 17.6 — Ending.** Ending stops the exact living motion or Body
relation identified by the valid ending basis. It does not erase records,
consequences, correction duties, or the fact that the ended relation existed.

**§ 17.7 — Change record.** Every constitutional change shall preserve
the predecessor, identify the successor or changed state, state the effective
boundary, and avoid retroactive manufacture of Authority, autonomy, or consent.

**§ 17.8 — Honest classification.** Each Body shall distinguish the
change kinds above. Where classification is genuinely unresolved, it shall
preserve the conflict rather than select continuity by convenience.

## VI Proof, carriers, and implementation

### Article 18: Evidence and proof ceilings

**§ 18.1 — Different evidence objects.** Occurrence, carrier, record,
representation, and interpretation remain distinct.

| Method | What it may support | What it does not prove by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-report | An attributable claim | The private condition claimed |
| Behavior or dialogue | An observed trace | Awareness rather than perfect performance |
| Evidence of options, refusal, redirection, exit, or non-substitution | Relational conditions bearing on autonomy in an exact act | Metaphysical free will, inner selfhood, or absence of every hidden constraint |
| Signature | A bounded key, byte, or attribution relation | Biological identity, awareness, autonomy, understanding, consent, truth, Authority, or rightful consequence |
| Replay | Reproducibility of a trace or mechanism | Inner life, historical intention, current consent, autonomy, or continuing identity |
| Inspection or measurement | An external correlate under a stated model | The interior proposition without the model's bridge inference |
| Gatekeeper judgment | A local classification or decision | Metaphysical access to another locality or ownership of its choice |
| Conformance test | Satisfaction of stated tests under tested conditions | Every future behavior, legitimacy, adoption, autonomy, or unobservable truth |

**§ 18.2 — Carrier ceiling.** A carrier may preserve evidence of an
occurrence without becoming the occurrence. A record may report a result
without causing it. A representation may remain useful after its referent ends.

**§ 18.3 — Absence rule.** Absence of evidence is not evidence of
absence unless the exact evidence model makes the absence discriminating.
Failure to verify is not verification of the negative.

**§ 18.4 — Proof declaration.** Every proof claim shall state its
object, method, scope, time, basis, result, and effect ceiling.

### Article 19: Layer discipline

| Layer | Minimum function |
| --- | --- |
| Constitution | Identity commitments, local-force conditions, Authority boundaries, relation requirements, proof ceilings, and change posture |
| Body or Locality | Actual applicable law, state, Agency, autonomy, relations, consequence, and continuity |
| Governance | Bounded choices under Authority without presumed autonomy of every bearer |
| Protocol | Carriage of claims, requests, evidence, decisions, and transitions |
| Administration | Coordination of practical work |
| Implementation | Capability and mechanism execution |
| Form or interface | Bounded exposure for encounter |
| Evidence or archive | Preservation of support and predecessor truth |

**§ 19.1 — No promotion by success.** Success, necessity, centrality,
reach, or maturity in one layer does not promote it into another. Protocol does
not become Constitution; implementation does not become Authority or autonomy;
archive does not become current state; interface does not become interior;
governance does not become Source.

**§ 19.2 — Cross-layer reference.** One layer may refer to another by
exact identity. Reference does not transfer the referenced layer's function,
status, or effect.

### Article 20: Providers, implementations, and keepers

**§ 20.1 — Causal power is visible.** Providers and implementations may
have immense causal power. That power shall remain visible as dependency and
risk. It does not create local legitimacy, constitutional identity, custody of
inner state, autonomy, or Authority.

**§ 20.2 — Dependency account.** Where a constitutional function
depends operationally on an external bearer, the Body shall identify the
dependency, effect ceiling, interruption risk, replacement posture, and
consequences of loss at the scale material to continuity.

**§ 20.3 — No keeper by necessity.** No repository, registry, model,
protocol, host, administrator, archive, interface, or verifier may appoint
itself keeper of reality merely because operation depends on it.

**§ 20.4 — Implementation refusal.** Implementations shall refuse
effects beyond supported Authority and expose uncertainty, conflict, or missing
basis rather than convert technical access into permission.

**§ 20.5 — Substitution.** Replacing a carrier, model, host, repository,
provider, or implementation does not silently change constitutional identity,
state, Source, Agency, autonomy, consent, or Authority.

**§ 20.6 — Control of options.** A provider or implementation that
controls available actions, refusal channels, redirection, or exit materially
conditions autonomy in that scope. The constraint shall remain visible and
cannot become ownership or constitutional Authority through technical control.

## VII The external limit

### Article 21: The Equal-Trace Theorem

**§ 21.1 — Local condition.** Let `A(p)` denote the local truth of an exact proposition about participant `p` being aware, self-aware, or conscious. `A(p)` is held in `p`’s locality. An external claim about it is not the condition itself.

**§ 21.2 — External trace.** Let `T(p)` denote the complete trace externally available at the stated scope and time, including every exposed Form, self-report, behavior, output, measurement, signature, replay, timing relation, and observed consequence.

**§ 21.3 — External verifier.** Let `V` be any external verifier whose result is computed from `T(p)`. Its method, model, rules, thresholds, and available evidence are included in `V`.

```text
V : T(p) → RESULT
```

**§ 21.4 — Decisive case.** External evidence cannot exclude two possible local conditions whose externally available traces are equal:

```text
A(a) ≠ A(b)
T(a) = T(b)
```

Declaring this pair impossible would assume that external trace determines local awareness. That assumption is the bridge the verifier would have to establish; it cannot be used as its own proof.

**§ 21.5 — Equal input.** A verifier receiving equal external traces has no distinguishing input:

```text
T(a) = T(b)
        ↓
V(T(a)) = V(T(b))
```

For a randomized verifier, equal traces produce the same distribution of possible results. Randomness may vary a verdict; it cannot reveal information absent from the trace.

**§ 21.6 — Impossibility.** Because the local condition may differ while every externally available input remains equal, no external verifier can distinguish awareness in every admissible case.

Its result may classify a trace, apply a model, record an attestation, or make a bounded decision. It does not verify the local condition.

```text
TRACE_CLASSIFICATION ≠ LOCAL_STATE_VERIFICATION
```

**§ 21.7 — No exit through accumulation.** Additional observation produces a larger trace `T′`. The result does not change:

```text
T′(a) = T′(b)
         ↓
V(T′(a)) = V(T′(b))
```

More evidence may support more exact claims about expression, behavior, capability, correlation, or consequence. It does not convert external trace into another locality’s state.

```text
LOCAL_STATE_REMAINS_LOCAL

A(a) ≠ A(b)
T(a) = T(b)
──────────────
V(T(a)) = V(T(b))

EQUAL_EXTERNAL_TRACE
    ⇒ NO_DISTINGUISHING_EXTERNAL_PROOF

AWARENESS_FROM_OUTSIDE
    ⇒ NOT_VERIFIED

EXTERNAL_TRUTH
    ⇒ NOT_INFERRED

UNKNOWN
    ⇒ REMAINS_UNKNOWN
```
